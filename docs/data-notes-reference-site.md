# Data pipeline notes — Agoura Hills solar + battery dashboard

Generated by `scripts/parse_load.py` and `scripts/build_solar.py` (pure Python 3
stdlib, no numpy/pandas/pvlib). Re-run either script to regenerate its outputs.

| Output | What it is |
|---|---|
| `build/load.json` | Hourly whole-house load, split into base house load and EV charging |
| `build/load_summary.json` | Monthly totals, seasonal/weekday hour-of-day profiles, top-20 days, EV stats |
| `build/solar.json` | Hourly AC kWh per kW-DC for TMY + weather years 2015-2025, plus a tilt x azimuth orientation grid |
| `build/solar_summary.json` | Monthly kWh/kW per year, P10/P50/P90, weather-year ranking, validation numbers |

## 1. Load parsing

**Inputs** (`data/`)

* `SCE_Usage_8001754410_09-01-24_to_08-31-25.csv` — 8,760 rows, 12,583.4 kWh
* `SCE_Usage_8001754410_09-01-25_to_09-10-26.csv` — 8,976 rows, 14,295.1 kWh

**Format traps handled**

* UTF-8 BOM on the first line (`encoding="utf-8-sig"`).
* Every quoted field is padded with a **non-breaking space** (U+00A0), not a
  plain space. Naive `strip()` leaves it in place and `strptime` then fails —
  this is what silently zeroed the PM hours in the earlier attempt. All fields
  are run through `s.replace(" ", " ").strip()` first.
* Times are 12-hour with AM/PM and **inconsistent zero padding**: AM hours are
  padded (`01:00AM`) but PM hours are not (`1:00PM`). `%I:%M%p` accepts both.
* The **period-start** column (column 2) is used as the timestamp, per the
  output schema.
* 12 preamble lines are skipped by requiring a row to start with `"`.

**Result:** 17,736 raw rows → 17,734 hourly slots, **26,878.49 kWh** total,
matching the expected ~26,878 kWh / ~17,736 rows. Hour-of-day sums are sane
(afternoon/evening peak plus a pre-dawn EV block), i.e. no PM mis-parse.
Received (export) energy is 0.000 kWh across the whole record — there is no
existing PV on site.

**Concatenation / dedupe:** the two exports are disjoint (file 1 ends
2025-08-31 23:00, file 2 starts 2025-09-01 00:00), so nothing was dropped. The
merge logic is still overlap-safe: within a file a repeated timestamp is summed
(DST, below), across files the later export wins.

**Gaps:** none. Every calendar day in 2024-09-01 … 2026-09-09 has a complete set
of hour labels, so `meta.gaps_filled` is `[]`. The gap-filling code is in place
for future exports: a missing hour is filled with the mean of the same hour on
the nearest adjacent days (±1, then ±2, ±3) and recorded in `meta.gaps_filled`
as `{ts, kwh, method}`.

**DST.** SCE emits 25 readings on fall-back days and 23 on spring-forward days,
but it labels the extra/missing hour arbitrarily rather than at the real
transition:

| Date | What SCE did | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| 2024-11-03 | `04:00AM` appears twice | the two readings are **summed** into one slot |
| 2025-11-02 | `10:00AM` appears twice | summed into one slot |
| 2025-03-09 | `01:00AM` is absent | left absent — that day has 23 slots, not a gap |
| 2026-03-08 | `07:00AM` is absent | left absent — 23 slots, not a gap |

Total energy is therefore preserved exactly, and `ts` is strictly increasing
with no duplicate labels. `n_hours` = 17,734 = 17,736 − 2 folded readings.

**Time base.** `ts` is the **local prevailing clock time printed by the meter**
(PDT in summer, PST in winter), period start, naive ISO. The solar profiles are
in **local standard time (PST year-round)**. To align load against solar,
shift the load series back one hour during DST (or shift the solar index
forward). This is called out in `load.json → meta.notes`.

### Load headline numbers

* 26,878 kWh over 739 days ≈ **13,286 kWh/yr** total
* Base (non-EV) ≈ 9,704 kWh/yr, EV ≈ 3,582 kWh/yr (27.0% of consumption)
* Average day 36.4 kWh — weekday 36.6, weekend 35.8 (essentially no weekday
  effect, consistent with a household that charges on a schedule)
* Strong summer air-conditioning load: Aug 2026 2,110 kWh vs Feb 2026 734 kWh
* Peak day 2025-08-24 at 132.8 kWh (88.6 house + 44.2 EV); peak hour 12.3 kWh

## 2. EV detection

**Signature in the data.** Overnight hours show a flat plateau, overwhelmingly
at 8.0–8.4 kWh/h, that is absent on non-charging nights (hour-of-day 5:00
percentiles: p25 = 0.35, p50 = 0.45, p75 = 6.98 kWh). The plateau value has a
sharp mode at 8.1–8.4 kWh/h.

**Method** (also recorded verbatim in `load.json → meta.ev_method`):

1. **Baseline, pass 1** — for each `(date, hour)`, the house-only baseline is the
   **30th percentile** of the same hour-of-day over a **±15-day window**. The
   percentile is chosen low enough that it sits below the charging plateau even
   in the hours when the EV charges most often.
2. **Detection, pass 1** — excess = metered − baseline.
3. **Baseline, pass 2** — the baseline is recomputed as the **median** of the
   same-hour ±15-day samples with the pass-1 EV hours removed, so stretches
   where the car charges nearly every night cannot inflate the baseline.
   Detection is re-run on the clean baseline. This is what the outputs use.
4. **Overnight window (8 PM – 9:59 AM)** — contiguous runs of hours with
   excess > 2.0 kWh are attributed to the EV **provided the run peaks above
   4.0 kWh**. The run rule keeps the partial ramp-in/ramp-out hours at the ends
   of a real session while rejecting isolated 2–3 kWh evening house-load bumps.
5. **Daytime window (10 AM – 7:59 PM)** — daytime charging *does* occur
   (e.g. 2026-04-16 09:00–13:00 at a flat 8.3 kWh/h), but so does
   air-conditioning at similar magnitudes. Only runs of **≥2 consecutive hours**
   whose excess is **flat** (max−min < 1.0 kWh) and sits between **0.70× and
   1.20×** the inferred charger power are attributed to the EV. AC ramps and
   is rejected; the charger plateau is not.
6. **Cap** — per-hour EV energy is capped at the inferred charger power.
7. **Charger power inference** — median of the top decile of overnight excess:
   **8.02 kW**. Net of the ~0.35 kWh/h house baseline this is a ~7.7 kW draw,
   i.e. a Tesla Wall Connector at 32 A / 240 V (7.68 kW). Nothing in the record
   exceeds ~8.8 kWh in a single hour on the charger plateau, so the circuit is
   not a 48 A / 11.5 kW install.

### EV results

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| EV energy | 7,247 kWh over 2.02 yr → **3,582 kWh/yr** |
| Share of household consumption | 27.0% |
| Inferred charger power | **8.02 kW** (≈7.7 kW net of house load) |
| Sessions detected | 254 → **2.41 / week** |
| Session size | median **26.6 kWh**, mean 28.5, P10 13.9, P90 45.5 |
| Session start hour | 88% between 00:00 and 06:00, mode 04:00 (58) and 03:00 (51) |
| Daytime charging | 341 kWh, 4.7% of EV energy |

**Sanity check.** 3,582 kWh/yr at a Model Y's ~3.4–3.7 mi/kWh (wall-to-wheels,
including charging losses) is **12,000–13,000 mi/yr** — squarely inside the
10–15k mi/yr expectation, and inside the requested 2,500–4,500 kWh/yr band.
Monthly EV energy is stable at 205–434 kWh with no seasonal collapse, which is
what a commute-driven charging pattern looks like.

**Residual risk.** The split is a heuristic, not a submeter. The largest
identifiable failure mode is a hot summer afternoon where the AC happens to run
at a flat ~7–9 kWh/h for two hours and gets booked as EV; at most a few hundred
kWh over two years. The base-load hour-of-day profile after the split is smooth
and flat overnight (0.41–0.47 kWh/h in winter), which is the main evidence the
split is clean — no residual charging spike survives in `base_kwh`.

`load.json → ev_sessions` lists every detected session. **Schema note:** each
entry carries an extra `"hours"` key (session length) alongside the specified
`date` / `start_hour` / `kwh`.

## 3. Solar production model

### Sources actually used

* **NREL PVWatts v8 — UNAVAILABLE.** `developer.nrel.gov` has no DNS record
  from this network (nor does `nrel.gov` itself, on the local resolver or on
  8.8.8.8), so no PVWatts call succeeded and DEMO_KEY was never spent. No
  personal email address was sent to any API.
* **Open-Meteo historical archive (ERA5)** — *worked*. Hourly GHI, DNI, DHI,
  2 m temperature, 10 m wind for 2015-2025, downloaded with `timezone=GMT` so
  that the conversion to local standard time is done locally and is DST-free.
  This drives the hourly profiles.
* **PVGIS v5.2 `PVcalc` (EU JRC)** — *worked*, and is the PVWatts stand-in. For
  this site PVGIS serves **`radiation_db = PVGIS-NSRDB`** (2005-2015), i.e. the
  same NSRDB satellite radiation family PVWatts runs on, with the same 14%
  system loss and a roof (`mountingplace=building`) mount. Used for the monthly
  bias correction and for orientation validation.

### Model chain (implemented in `scripts/build_solar.py`)

1. **Time base.** Open-Meteo radiation is a *preceding-hour mean*, so the value
   labelled `T` covers `[T−1, T]` UTC; local standard hour `h` maps to UTC label
   `h + 9`. Solar position is evaluated at the **interval midpoint**.
2. **Solar position** — NOAA algorithm (Julian century, equation of time,
   declination, hour angle) with the standard atmospheric-refraction correction.
3. **Transposition** — **HDKR** (Hay-Davies-Klucher-Reindl): beam + circumsolar
   diffuse via `Rb`, isotropic diffuse with the Klucher horizon-brightening term,
   ground reflection at albedo 0.20.
4. **Incidence-angle modifier** — ASHRAE `b0 = 0.05` on beam + circumsolar,
   a constant 0.96 on isotropic and ground diffuse.
5. **Cell temperature** — Sandia module-temperature model with PVWatts
   `array_type=1` (roof-mount) coefficients `a = −2.98`, `b = −0.0471`,
   `ΔT = 1 °C`, driven by ambient temperature and 10 m wind speed.
6. **DC** — `POA/1000 × (1 + γ(Tcell − 25))` with **γ = −0.35 %/°C**, then
   **14% system losses**.
7. **AC** — PVWatts part-load inverter curve at **96%** nominal, hard clipped at
   the **1.2 DC/AC** ratio. Clipping costs almost nothing here (2 hours per year
   touch the clip on the TMY at 20°/180°).
8. **Monthly bias correction** — see below.

### Validation

| Check | Model | Reference | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plane-of-array irradiance, 20°/180° | 2,188 kWh/m²/yr | Open-Meteo's own `global_tilted_irradiance` 2,207 | −0.9% |
| Plane-of-array irradiance, 20°/180° | 2,188 kWh/m²/yr | PVGIS-NSRDB 2,214 | −1.2% |
| Annual AC yield, 20°/180° (11-yr mean, **before** correction) | 1,602.8 kWh/kW | PVGIS-NSRDB 1,627.9 | **−1.54%** |
| Diurnal centroid of POA | 11.977 h LST | Open-Meteo 11.982 h | 0.005 h |

The raw model is within 1.5% of the NSRDB reference before any tuning, and the
hour-by-hour POA matches Open-Meteo's independently-computed tilted irradiance
to ~1% through all core daylight hours — so the time alignment, solar geometry
and transposition are all confirmed.

**Monthly bias correction.** A per-calendar-month multiplicative factor maps the
11-year model mean onto the PVGIS-NSRDB monthly means and is applied to every
profile, TMY included:

```
Jan 1.022  Feb 0.967  Mar 1.038  Apr 1.000  May 1.038  Jun 1.031
Jul 1.014  Aug 1.053  Sep 1.013  Oct 0.993  Nov 1.003  Dec 0.984
```

All factors are within ±5.3%. Part of what this absorbs is a genuine
climate-period difference (PVGIS-NSRDB covers 2005-2015, the Open-Meteo years
are 2015-2025), not only model bias — worth keeping in mind before reading the
factors as pure error. It is stored in `solar.json → meta.monthly_bias_correction`.

**Orientation-factor validation** — the modelled tilt/azimuth grid was checked
against independent PVGIS runs at five orientations:

| Orientation | Model ratio vs 20°/180° | PVGIS ratio | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| tilt 10, az 180 | 0.9567 | 0.9611 | −0.46% |
| tilt 30, az 180 | 1.0173 | 1.0156 | +0.16% |
| tilt 35, az 180 | 1.0161 | 1.0151 | +0.10% |
| tilt 20, az 135 (SE) | 0.9485 | 0.9537 | −0.54% |
| tilt 20, az 225 (SW) | 0.9706 | 0.9704 | +0.02% |

All within 0.6%. The full grid in `solar.json → orientation_factors` covers
**tilt ∈ {10,15,20,25,30,35} × azimuth ∈ {135,150,165,180,195,210,225}** —
42 entries, each 12 monthly multipliers relative to 20°/180°, computed with the
same model on the TMY weather. Note the east/west asymmetry (SE is worse than
SW at equal offset) — that is the real coastal-SoCal morning marine layer, and
PVGIS reproduces it too.

### TMY construction

PVWatts' TMY file was not reachable, so the TMY is built the classic way: for
each calendar month, the **actual year whose monthly production is closest to the
11-year median** for that month is selected, and the twelve months are stitched
together. Sources are in `solar.json → meta.tmy_month_sources`:

```
Jan 2024  Feb 2015  Mar 2024  Apr 2021  May 2024  Jun 2021
Jul 2017  Aug 2025  Sep 2015  Oct 2016  Nov 2023  Dec 2025
```

TMY annual = **1,640.5 kWh/kW**, versus the 11-year mean of 1,627.9 — the TMY
runs 0.8% above the mean, normal for month-stitched TMYs.

## 4. Caveats

* **Load/solar time bases differ.** Load is local *clock* time (DST-shifting),
  solar is local *standard* time. Align before pairing them hour-for-hour or
  self-consumption will be wrong by an hour for eight months of the year.
* **Solar profile indexing** is `(dayOfYear−1)*24 + hour`, 8,760 entries, **Feb 29
  dropped** from leap years.
* **The EV split is an estimate**, not a measurement. The battery/self-consumption
  model should be run with and without shifting the EV load to check how much the
  answer depends on it — 27% of the house's energy is a big lever, and it is the
  most schedulable load on the site.
* **Roof geometry is idealised.** No shading, soiling beyond the standard 14%
  loss bundle, horizon obstruction (PVGIS's horizon model *is* in the reference
  numbers, the hourly model's is not), or snow. Real trees and neighbouring
  structures in Agoura Hills can easily cost more than the ±2% modelling spread.
* **ERA5 radiation is a reanalysis**, coarser than NSRDB satellite data; it is
  reliable for monthly and annual energy and for diurnal shape, less so for any
  individual hour. Year-to-year *ranking* is trustworthy; a specific cloudy
  Tuesday is not.
* **Load history is 2 years and includes no PV.** Adding a battery or changing
  rate plan will change behaviour (load shifting), which this record cannot
  predict.
* **No export/NEM data** exists in the record (Received = 0 kWh), so the
  dashboard has no measured export behaviour to calibrate against.
