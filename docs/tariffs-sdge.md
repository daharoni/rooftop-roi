## Net Billing export

Vintage NBT26, lock-in 9 years, NSC $0.01702/kWh, CPA-style export adder $0/kWh.

WHAT THESE NUMBERS ARE. Under the Net Billing Tariff (SDG&E brands it the Solar Billing Plan; everyone else says NEM 3.0) exported energy is credited at hourly Avoided Cost Calculator values, not at the retail rate. The export_rates matrices are SDG&E's own published export prices for the NBT26 vintage, calendar year 2026, taken from its CPUC Resolution E-5301 machine-readable MIDAS compliance file, which already carries the CPUC-mandated month / day-type / hour aggregation in its ValueName field - 576 distinct cells per component with zero conflicting values, so nothing was interpolated or re-averaged. Hour index is hour-BEGINNING 0-23 in Pacific PREVAILING time, DST aware; the weekend table applies on Saturdays, Sundays and the eight holidays. Each value is the TOTAL credit = generation component + delivery component; a CCA customer receives the delivery component from SDG&E and the generation component from the CCA, and the sum is unchanged. The split is preserved in data/export/sdge-nbt26.json. SHAPE. Peak: August weekday hour 18 (6-7 p.m.) = $1.06977/kWh, hour 20 = $0.98482, hour 17 = $0.92495; August weekend hour 20 = $1.01965. Trough: spring weekend midday reaches $0.00000. The midday mean over hours 9-15 is $0.04202 weekday and $0.02037 weekend, against an unweighted all-hours mean of $0.09274 weekday and $0.08395 weekend. Use the midday figure as the anchor for unpaired solar: the all-hours mean is carried by a handful of extreme August-evening cells a PV array alone never reaches. Exporting a midday kWh earns roughly a tenth of what importing one costs on TOU-DR1, so self-consumption and evening battery discharge carry essentially all of the value. SDG&E's curve tracks PG&E's and SCE's closely - September weekday hour 19 is $0.58179 here against PG&E's $0.59505 - which is expected, since all three derive from the same 2024 ACC under one CPUC methodology. NO ACC PLUS ADDER. This is the single biggest difference between SDG&E and the other two California IOUs for a solar customer, and it is easy to miss. CPUC D.22-12-056 created a glide-path adder paid on top of the ACC export price - PG&E residential gets $0.0088/kWh for a 2026 vintage, SCE gets $0.016 - but the decision granted it ONLY to PG&E and SCE residential customers. SDG&E customers receive NOTHING. acc_plus_adder_per_kwh is therefore a real 0.0, not a placeholder. Confidence MEDIUM rather than HIGH: SDG&E's tariff viewer is a JavaScript application from which the Schedule NBT PDF could not be retrieved, so this rests on the decision text plus the complete absence of any adder provision in SDG&E's own Solar Billing Plan materials. THE NINE-YEAR LOCK-IN FREEZES A TRAJECTORY, NOT A FLAT TABLE. A 2026 vintage locks NBT26 for nine years, but that vintage specifies a different 12x24 matrix for every year: the generation component decays after 2028 while the delivery component grows. This file carries the 2026 matrix only. The direction of the error is documented in detail in data/tari

## Method notes

RATE MODEL. Every rates[season][period][provider] value is the FULL retail $/kWh that provider's customer pays in that hour. SDG&E's own columns are transcribed verbatim from SDG&E's published Total Rates Tables effective 2026-08-01, which also print the component split - so UDC Total + Schedule WF-NBC/DWR-BC ($0.00591) + Schedule EECC re-sums to the published total in every one of the 26 season/period cells of this file (verified programmatically, zero mismatches). HOW THE CCA COLUMNS WERE BUILT. SDG&E's Schedule EECC rate is generation INCLUDING the bundled customer's share of the PCIA - the joint rate comparisons split it, showing TOU-DR1 as generation $0.12181 + PCIA $0.04988 = $0.17169, which is exactly the class-average EECC. A CCA customer does not pay EECC at all: they pay SDG&E's delivery plus their own CCA's generation plus their own CCA's PCIA. So cca_total = (UDC + WF-NBC/DWR-BC) + cca_generation + cca_pcia. Applying that identity to the joint rate comparison's own class averages reproduces its published totals to $0.0001, which is what validates the construction. THE NUMBER THAT LOOKS WRONG AND IS NOT: CEA's TOU-DR1 summer on-peak total is $0.91302/kWh, $0.22 above SDG&E's $0.69135. That is real and it comes straight off CEA's own adopted rate schedule, which prices summer on-peak generation at $0.55397/kWh against SDG&E's $0.35943. CEA's rates are far more TOU-differentiated than SDG&E's in BOTH directions: its winter on-peak generation is $0.19791 against SDG&E's $0.28279, so a CEA customer pays much more in the summer evening and noticeably less all winter. Blended over a year at CEA's own published class-average usage the two land within about 3 cents, matching the +6.8% their joint rate comparison reports. For a solar-plus-battery household this shape is not simply bad news: it makes every kWh shifted out of the 4-9 p.m. summer window worth substantially more. SDCP IS VINTAGE-NEUTRAL, CEA IS NOT. SDCP publishes separate 2021-vintage and 2022-vintage generation sheets that differ by exactly the PCIA difference ($0.00559/kWh), so generation + PCIA is identical for every SDCP customer and the single sdcp column here is correct for all of them. CEA publishes ONE generation schedule but spans three PCIA vintages, because its cities joined in different years: Carlsbad, Del Mar and Solana Beach in 2021 ($0.03348/kWh), Escondido and San Marcos in 2023 ($0.02613), Oceanside and Vista in 2024 ($0.04840). This file uses $0.02613. FLAGGED AS DERIVED: CEA's own 2026-06-01 joint rate comparison shows a single PCIA of $0.02827, and the system-wide PCIA fell $0.00217/kWh between the June and August tables (SDCP's 2021 vintage moved $0.03565 to $0.03348 over the same interval), which puts CEA's August figure at $0.02610 - the 2023 vintage to within $0.00003. A CEA customer in Oceanside or Vista may therefore pay about $0.022/kWh MORE than this file's cea column suggests, and one in Carlsbad, Del Mar or Solana Beach about $0.007 more. Confidence MEDIUM. THE SUPER-OFF-PEAK WINDOW MOVED, AND ONE PLAN IS CONTESTED. Effective 2026-05-01 SDG&E made the weekday 10 a.m.-2 p.m. super-off-peak window YEAR-ROUND; it had previously applied in MARCH AND APRIL ONLY. That change is in the base weekday schedules of TOU-DR1, TOU-ELEC and EV-TOU-5 here. EV-TOU-2 is the exception: SDG&E's own list of affected schedules omits it and its tariff sheet still reads March and April only, so EV-TOU-2 carries the narrow window through plans[].schedule_overrides instead. Both SDCP's and CEA's current rate sheets present the year-round window as applying to every schedule they serve, including EV-TOU-2, so the evidence conflicts. Confidence LOW on EV-TOU-2 specifically; HIGH on the other four. SEASONS. Summer is JUNE 1 - OCTOBER 31, five months, on every plan - a month longer than PG&E's and SCE's four-month June-September summer. Winter is November 1 - May 31. HOLIDAYS. The weekend schedule applies on the eight holidays in Electric Rule 1: New Year's Day, Presidents' Day, Memorial Day, Independence Day, Labor Day, Veterans Day, Thanksgiving and Christmas. SDG&E observes a Sunday holiday on the following Monday. core/tariff.js also observes a Saturday holiday on the preceding Friday, which is the federal convention and which SDG&E's rule does not adopt; the difference affects at most a couple of days a year and always in the customer's favour. PERIOD COVERAGE AND FILLER. period_ids declares all four ids on every plan so that any [season][period] lookup resolves, but SDG&E has NO residential mid-peak or part-peak period at all, so every mid cell in this file is filler carrying the on-peak value. TOU-DR2 has no super-off-peak either, so its super_off cells carry its off-peak value. The 24-hour schedule arrays never emit a filler id, so a correct simulation never reads one. BASELINE. TOU-DR1 and TOU-DR2 carry a $0.10702/kWh credit on usage up to 130% - not 100% - of the daily baseline allocation. TOU-ELEC, EV-TOU-5 and EV-TOU-2 carry none. The rates in this file are PRE-CREDIT, as in the SCE and PG&E files. THE BASELINE ZIP HINTS ARE INFERENCE, NOT TARIFF, AND WEAKER HERE THAN ANYWHERE ELSE IN THE LIBRARY. SDG&E publishes its Coastal / Inland / Mountain / Desert boundaries only as 'Territory Served, Map No. 1' and an interactive ArcGIS map - there is no table, and no ZIP list, at any level. The hints here are ordinary San Diego County geography (Alpine, Julian, Pine Valley and the back-country ZIPs as Mountain; Borrego Springs as Desert; the El Cajon, Escondido, Poway, Santee, San Marcos and Vista valleys as Inland; everything else Coastal) and are LOW confidence. A specific warning: SDG&E ALSO publishes a document called 'Utility Zip Codes by Climate Zones'. That file lists CEC Title 24 BUILDING climate zones 1-16 and has nothing to do with baseline allowances - do not use it for this. SDG&E's own baseline allowance calculator is the authoritative lookup. The allocation values themselves come from Schedule DR Sheet 5, whose sheet was last revised in December 2017; they match every third-party citation found, but their currency is MEDIUM rather than HIGH. Medical Baseline adds 16.5 kWh/day. FIXED CHARGE. $0.79343/day standard - identical to PG&E's to the fifth decimal, because both implement the same CPUC income-graduated fixed charge under AB 205 and D.24-05-028. $0.39688/day for FERA and deed-restricted affordable housing, $0.19713/day for CARE. Effective October 2025. It REPLACED the residential minimum bill, so minimum_charge_per_day is a real 0.00, and it also replaced the $16/month basic service fee that EV-TOU-5 and TOU-ELEC used to carry - SDG&E's own TOU-ELEC plan page still advertises that dead fee. The only residential schedule that still has a minimum bill is Schedule EV-TOU, the separately metered EV service, at $0.413/day; it is not in this file. CHARGES NOT IN THE PER-kWh RATES. Franchise fees are excluded from every column, exactly as SDG&E's own Total Rates Tables exclude them: a CCA customer pays a 1.1% franchise fee surcharge on generation supplied by others, and EVERY customer inside the City of San Diego pays a further 5.78% franchise fee differential as a separate bill line. Add them on top if you are reproducing a bill to the cent; together they are well under 1% of the total for a customer outside the City of San Diego. Note the asymmetry with data/tariffs/pge.json, which DOES include the CCA franchise fee surcharge - in PG&E territory the CCAs publish their comparisons inclusive of it, and each file follows its own utility's published convention. ZIP PREFIXES. 919, 920 and 921 are San Diego County. 926 is southern Orange County - San Clemente, San Juan Capistrano, Dana Point, Laguna Niguel - and OVERLAPS with SCE, which serves the rest of 926xx. utilityForZip() returns both with ambiguous:true and the UI must ask. The 926 entries were derived from a SDG&E document that mixes gas and electric service, so some 926xx ZIPs in it (92649 Huntington Beach, 92697 UC Irvine) are almost certainly SCE for electricity; treat 926xx as needing per-address confirmation. One outlier ZIP, 92536 (Aguanga, Riverside County), is omitted.
